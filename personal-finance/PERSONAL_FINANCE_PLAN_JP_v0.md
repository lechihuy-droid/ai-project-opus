## 2. Consultant Framework Selected

### Recommended framework

**UBS SuMi TRUST-style wealth planning + CFP-style discipline.**

Reason:

- UBS SuMi TRUST gives the high-level private-banking mindset: wealth preservation, goal-based allocation, risk control, cross-border awareness, long-term family/asset planning.
- CFP-style planning ensures the base is not skipped: cashflow, emergency fund, insurance, pension, tax, behavioral discipline.
- For a non-ultra-high-net-worth individual, this framework can be used without buying private banking products.

### Practical implementation channel

Actual implementation should likely use ordinary Japan-accessible platforms first:

- bank account / savings account
- SBI / Rakuten / Monex or equivalent brokerage
- New NISA
- iDeCo, only if suitable after liquidity and long-term Japan/retirement assumptions are checked
- low-cost diversified funds rather than high-fee products, unless a clear reason exists

## 3. Core Planning Principles

### 3.1 Foundation before optimization

Order of decision:

1. cashflow clarity
2. emergency fund
3. high-interest debt elimination
4. insurance/protection gap check
5. tax-advantaged investing
6. taxable investing
7. advanced products / real estate / business investment

### 3.2 Japan-specific awareness

The plan must account for:

- yen-based income and expenses
- Japan pension/social insurance system
- residence tax timing
- potential bonus-based salary structure
- visa / residency / long-term stay uncertainty
- possibility of future Vietnam return or cross-border financial obligations
- JPY / VND / USD currency exposure

### 3.3 No product-first planning

Do not start with questions like:

- “Which stock should I buy?”
- “Should I max NISA immediately?”
- “Should I buy real estate?”

Start with:

- What is the goal?
- What is the time horizon?
- What is the downside capacity?
- What must not fail?

## 6. Emergency Fund Policy

### Target

Default target:

```text
Emergency fund = 6 months of essential expenses
```

Use 9–12 months if:

- job is unstable
- visa/residence risk exists
- dependents rely on income
- income is bonus-heavy or project-based
- user plans business transition

### Storage

Emergency fund should be held in low-risk liquid form:

- Japanese bank savings account
- partial cash reserve if necessary
- not in volatile investment assets

### Rule

Do not invest aggressively until at least 3 months of essential expenses is secured.

## 7. Debt Strategy

### Debt classification

| Debt type | Action |
|---|---|
| Credit card revolving / consumer finance | Highest priority payoff |
| Personal loan high interest | Aggressive payoff |
| Student loan / low-interest structured debt | Compare interest vs investment return |
| Mortgage | Treat as long-term planning decision |
| Family obligation | Define fixed monthly budget and boundary |

### Payoff order

1. minimum payments on all debts
2. eliminate high-interest debt
3. build emergency fund to 3 months
4. balance debt repayment vs investing
5. build emergency fund to 6–12 months

## 8. Japan System Layer

### 8.1 NISA

New NISA is the default first investment container for long-term investing in Japan because it provides tax-exempt investment treatment under the current scheme. It should be considered after cashflow and emergency fund basics are in place.

Decision questions:

- Is the user staying in Japan long enough to benefit?
- Does the user have stable monthly surplus?
- Should contributions be monthly automated or bonus-weighted?
- What should be the ratio between tsumitate-style long-term diversified funds and growth allocation?

Initial policy:

```text
If emergency fund >= 3 months and no high-interest debt:
    start NISA monthly contribution
Else:
    prioritize emergency fund/debt first
```

### 8.2 iDeCo

iDeCo can be attractive for retirement planning and tax deduction, but it has liquidity constraints. In principle, assets are not withdrawable until age 60, so it should not be used for money needed for near-term goals.

Decision questions:

- Is the user likely to stay in Japan long-term?
- Is the user comfortable locking money until retirement age?
- What is the user’s taxable income bracket?
- Does the employer offer corporate DC or other pension plan?
- Is the user prioritizing liquidity for business, relocation, marriage, house purchase, or family support?

Initial policy:

```text
Use iDeCo only after:
1. emergency fund is adequate
2. NISA habit is established
3. liquidity needs for the next 3–5 years are clear
4. long-term Japan/retirement assumption is acceptable
```

### 8.3 Pension / social insurance

Confirm the user’s enrollment status:

- Employees’ Pension Insurance if company employee
- National Pension if not covered by employee pension
- health insurance
- unemployment/employment insurance where applicable

The plan should not duplicate protection that already exists through public/social insurance unless there is a real gap.

### Objective

Avoid both under-insurance and over-insurance.

### Checkpoints

| Insurance type | Needed? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Health insurance | usually covered via Japan system | confirm employment status |
| Life insurance | only if dependents rely on income | avoid unnecessary policy if no dependents |
| Disability / income protection | important if income is critical | compare employer/social coverage |
| Cancer / medical add-on | case-by-case | avoid emotional purchase |
| Fire / earthquake insurance | relevant if renting/owning | check contract |
| Liability insurance | useful in Japan | check credit card/renter bundle |

### Principle

Insurance is for catastrophic risks, not for investment return.

## 16. Reference Notes

- New NISA was introduced in January 2024. The new system is permanent, has an indefinite tax-free holding period, and increased annual/lifetime limits according to the Japan Securities Dealers Association overview.
- iDeCo is an individual defined contribution pension. The official iDeCo site explains that participants decide how to invest their own contributions, can generally receive old-age benefits from age 60, and in principle cannot withdraw assets before age 60.
- Japan Pension Service explains that National Pension covers residents aged 20–59 and Employees’ Pension Insurance covers company/public-sector workers.
- Tax and deduction details should be checked against the latest National Tax Agency materials before finalizing any tax-sensitive action.
