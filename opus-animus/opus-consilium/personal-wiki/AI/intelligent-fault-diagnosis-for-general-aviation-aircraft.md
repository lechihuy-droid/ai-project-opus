# Intelligent Fault Diagnosis for General Aviation Aircraft

## Summary
This paper proposes an intelligent fault diagnosis framework for general aviation aircraft based on a multi-fidelity digital twin and FMEA knowledge enhancement. The framework integrates four modules: high-fidelity flight dynamics simulation, FMEA-driven fault injection, multi-fidelity residual feature extraction, and large language model (LLM)-enhanced interpretable report generation.

## Key Points
- The framework uses a digital twin constructed with the JSBSim six-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) flight dynamics engine.
- A three-layer fault injection engine based on failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA) models is used.
- The framework generates 23-channel engine health monitoring data via semi-empirical sensor synthesis equations.

## Why It Matters
This research is crucial for improving the safety and reliability of general aviation aircraft by providing an effective fault diagnosis method. It also highlights the potential of digital twins and LLMs in enhancing the maintenance and operation of complex systems.

## Details
The proposed framework is designed to address the challenges of scarce real fault data, diverse fault types, and weak fault signatures in general aviation aircraft. By leveraging the strengths of digital twins, FMEA, and LLMs, the framework provides a comprehensive and interpretable approach to fault diagnosis.

## Application To OPUS ANIMUS
The concepts and techniques presented in this research can be applied to the development of more sophisticated maintenance and operation systems for complex assets, such as those used in OPUS ANIMUS. The use of digital twins and LLMs can enhance the accuracy and efficiency of fault diagnosis, leading to improved overall system performance and reduced downtime.
