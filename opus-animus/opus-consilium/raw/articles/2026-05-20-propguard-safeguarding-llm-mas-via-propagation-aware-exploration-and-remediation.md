Abstract: LLM-based multi-agent systems (LLM-MAS) have become a promising paradigm for solving complex tasks through role specialization, tool use, memory, and collaborative reasoning. However, these interactions create new security risks that malicious instructions injected through messages, tools, or memories can propagate across agents and rounds, causing system-level compromise. Existing defenses largely rely on local filtering or graph-based anomaly detection, but they often fail to trace fine-grained propagation paths or remediate contaminated states without disrupting benign collaboration.
