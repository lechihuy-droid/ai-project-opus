## Pass Definition

An example passes when:

- Japanese is natural and grammar is correct;
- Japanese matches the intended scene, relationship, and register;
- Vietnamese sounds natural;
- Vietnamese public-facing wording is clearly better than the literal support version;
- literal support translation is available if needed;
- the example begins from a believable scene, not a grammar shell;
- persona fit is clear;
- emotional job is clear;
- grammar affordance is strong;
- exam transfer is visible;
- real-life transfer is visible;
- cringe risk is low or intentionally controlled;
- no real-person drama / unsafe reference is needed.

## Review Questions

Use these questions when reviewing a candidate:

1. Does the situation naturally create the target grammar logic?
2. Would the core persona understand the situation immediately?
3. Does the Vietnamese line sound like something a real person would say?
4. Does the Japanese line sound like something a real person in that scene would actually say?
5. Are the public-facing line and support line clearly doing different jobs?
6. Is there enough emotional tension to feel alive, but not enough to become drama?
7. Can this example transfer into exam reasoning or answer elimination?
8. Can this example also transfer into real-life understanding or usage?
9. Is this better than the strongest evergreen alternative?

---

## Quick Score

Use a simple 1-5 score for:

```text
Grammar fit
Persona fit
Natural Vietnamese
Natural Japanese
Exam transfer
Real-life transfer
Hook strength
Cringe risk control
```

Approval rule:

```text
Approve:
mostly 4-5 with no serious weakness

Approved with edit:
strong core but wording needs repair

Use only in shorts:
fresh hook but weaker long-form teaching value

Reject:
weak grammar fit or weak naturalness
```

---

## Automatic Fails

Reject or downgrade the candidate if:

- the scene is vague and exists only to host the grammar;
- Japanese is technically correct but sounds unnatural for the scene;
- Vietnamese sounds like a direct gloss instead of spoken language;
- the public-facing line and support line are basically the same sentence with minor word swaps;
- the example only exists to display the grammar and does not feel like a real communicative moment;
- the trend angle is interesting but weakens naturalness or grammar fit.
