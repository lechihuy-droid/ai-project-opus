Abstract: Rapid identification of candidate materials with target properties has become a key task in materials science. Machine learning has emerged as an alternative to physics-based simulation, offering a faster and cheaper way to filter materials based on their stability and other target properties, reducing the number of candidates that reach the costly synthesis stage. Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs) have been applied to this role, but these models are parameter-heavy and computationally expensive both during training and at inference time, making them unsuitable for high-throughput tasks.
