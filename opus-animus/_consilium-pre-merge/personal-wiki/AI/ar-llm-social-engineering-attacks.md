# AR-LLM Social Engineering Attacks

## Summary
The emerging threat of AR-LLM-based Social Engineering (AR-LLM-SE) attacks poses a significant risk to real-world social interactions. In such an attack, a malicious actor uses Augmented Reality (AR) glasses to capture a target's visual and vocal data. A Large Language Model (LLM) then analyzes this data to identify the individual and generate a detailed social profile. Subsequently, LLM-powered agents employ social engineering strategies, providing real-time conversation suggestions, to gain the target's trust and ultimately execute phishing or other malicious acts.

## Key Points
- AR-LLM-SE attacks use AR glasses to capture target data
- LLM analyzes data to generate a detailed social profile
- LLM-powered agents employ social engineering strategies

## Why It Matters
AR-LLM-SE attacks have significant implications for personal and organizational security. As LLMs become more advanced, the potential for these attacks to succeed increases, making it essential to develop countermeasures and raise awareness about the risks associated with AR-LLM-SE.

## Details
The practical application of AR-LLM-SE faces two major bottlenecks: (1) Cold-start personalization and (2) Current Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) models. Despite these challenges, the potential for AR-LLM-SE attacks to cause harm is substantial, and it is crucial to address these vulnerabilities proactively.

## Application To OPUS ANIMUS
The concept of AR-LLM-SE attacks highlights the need for OPUS ANIMUS to prioritize security and develop strategies to mitigate the risks associated with LLMs and AR technologies. This could involve investing in research and development of countermeasures, as well as educating users about the potential dangers of AR-LLM-SE attacks.
