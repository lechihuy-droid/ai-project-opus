## Summary
Requirement Driven Development (RDD) is a software development approach that focuses on building the right product by validating requirements early and verifying builds against those requirements. It involves constant testing and evaluation to ensure that the product meets the client's needs. The goal of RDD is to build the right thing, rather than just building it right.

## Key points
- Requirement Driven Development (RDD) focuses on building the right product
- Validation is a crucial step in RDD to ensure that the client can understand, agree, and benefit from what is being built
- Verification tests whether you built the thing right, while validation tests whether you built the right thing
- Test Driven Development (TDD) is a process for meeting the verification needs of a software project

## Details
RDD involves a formal Test and Evaluation (T&E) program to accomplish two goals: verification of the build components and validation of the program objectives. The easiest way to distinguish these goals is to remember that verification tests whether you built the thing right, while validation tests whether you built the right thing. Validation is a crucial step at every stage of a program to ensure that a client can understand, agree, and benefit from what is being built.
