# The Power of Power Law: Asymmetry Enables Compositional Reasoning

## Summary
Natural language data follows a power-law distribution, with most knowledge and skills appearing at very low frequency. Training under power-law distributions consistently outperforms training under uniform distributions in compositional reasoning tasks.

## Key Points
- Power-law distributions outperform uniform distributions in compositional reasoning tasks
- Training under power-law distributions requires significantly less training data

## Why It Matters
Understanding the power of power law is crucial for improving the performance of AI models in compositional reasoning tasks, which is essential for many real-world applications.

## Details
The paper introduces a minimalist skill-composition task and shows that learning under a power-law distribution provably requires significantly less training data. The theoretical analysis reveals that power law sampling enables compositional reasoning by allowing models to learn from rare but important events.

## Application To OPUS ANIMUS
The insights from this paper can be applied to improve the performance of AI models in OPUS ANIMUS, particularly in tasks that require compositional reasoning. By leveraging the power of power law, we can develop more efficient and effective AI systems.
