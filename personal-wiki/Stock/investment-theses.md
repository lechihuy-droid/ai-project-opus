# Investment Theses

## Summary
This page turns AI and macro sources into investment thinking. It is not a stock-picking page. It tracks theses, evidence, risks, and what would change the view.

## Key Points
- AI agents may shift software labor, enterprise budgets, and workflow platforms.
- Inference demand and reliability layers may be more durable than model hype.
- Competitor AI business models matter when they show durable monetization, delivery capacity, account control, or managed operations.
- Japan and APAC signals matter through industrial policy, supply chains, robotics, semiconductors, energy, enterprise adoption, and SIer modernization.
- A thesis is useful only if it states evidence, risk, time horizon, and disconfirming signals.
- 2026-05-20 update: AI-infra and Japan/APAC exposure got stronger as tracking buckets; individual equities still need separate valuation work before action.

## Why It Matters
Investment thinking needs discipline. The wiki should prevent chasing isolated headlines and instead track repeated signals.

## Details
Current thesis buckets:
- AI infra and inference demand
- Agentic software development and enterprise workflow platforms
- Evaluation, security, HITL, and governance tooling
- Competitor AI business models and SIer/consulting monetization patterns
- Japan/Asia supply-chain and industrial policy exposure
- AI-enabled productivity and labor restructuring

**Unchanged**
- Consumer-facing AI finance/search products like [[a-new-personal-finance-experience-in-chatgpt]], [[openai-launches-chatgpt-for-personal-finance-will-let-you-connect-bank-accounts]], and [[the-new-ai-powered-google-finance-is-expanding-to-europe]] are important adoption signals, but not enough by themselves to create an investment thesis.

**Risk Checks**
- Separate durable demand from capital-expenditure hype.
- Watch whether Japan AI-infra beneficiaries show margin expansion, backlog durability, pricing power, or only headline exposure.
- Treat AI labor-displacement stories as social/economic signals first, not automatic investment signals.
